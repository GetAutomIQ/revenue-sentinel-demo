What to look at

Five questions worth answering while you click through
Does the structure still match your workflow?

Nothing was removed or reordered. If a section belongs somewhere else, this is the moment.

Is the colour carrying the right meaning?

Mint = action and earned revenue. Blue = current / ADR. Lavender = future bookings and targets. Amber = needs review. Rose = error. The same meaning holds on every page.

Do the second layers answer the question you actually have?

Each one should explain what the number means, how it compares with target, and what is driving it.

Does it read on a phone?

Narrow the window below 980px. The sidebar becomes a drawer and every grid collapses.

Both themes?

The toggle is top right and is remembered between pages.

What replacing the Control Center would involve

So the approval is an informed one
This draft

Twelve static pages plus assets/rs.css and assets/rs.js, built by build/build.py. One page module per production generator, so the port is a move rather than a rewrite.

Charts are inline SVG drawn from the CSS custom properties, which is why a theme switch redraws them correctly and why nothing is fetched from a CDN.

The production port

The live Control Center is generated. Copying these files into site/ would look right until the next scheduled build overwrote them.

A real replacement means making cc_tokens.py the single token source and moving this markup into each cc_*_build.py, the three report builders and the live approvals API — done on a branch, with the current design one git checkout away.

The big picture · Claude prose (cached)
Quarter review
Claude prose · cached

This has been a strong quarter. Revenue for July through September came in comfortably ahead of target — around 40% above what had been planned for these three months — with July and August each doing exceptionally well against both the plan and the same months last year. September, which is not quite finished, has already reached its own target with only a day or so left to record, so the picture there is close enough to complete that it is unlikely to change much.

Underneath that headline, the shape of the quarter has been changing month by month, and the direction of travel matters as much as the total. Occupancy eased a little in each successive month, and the average rate the hotel achieved fell by more than half between July and September. That is a real pattern across all three months rather than a one-off dip, and it is the main thing worth carrying into the next quarter: whether it is a seasonal cooling that will pass, or something that continues.

There is also a concentration worth naming. Close to two-thirds of the rooms sold this quarter came through the hotel’s own direct channel, mostly bookings made straight through your website, with the ten-plus other channels the property uses making up a much smaller share between them individually. Being direct-heavy is, in one sense, a strength — you are not paying away margin to agents and are not exposed to any single OTA pulling back. But it does mean the business currently rests heavily on your own website converting well; if that channel softened, no other channel of comparable size is currently ready to take up the slack.

Two other patterns stand out in how guests are booking. How far ahead people book swung around a good deal from month to month with no clear direction — some months guests were booking well over three months out, others closer to two and a half — which makes it harder to plan staffing and pricing with confidence. Length of stay, on the other hand, moved more consistently: guests stayed a little less long with each month that passed through the quarter. Neither is alarming on its own, but together they suggest a guest base whose behaviour is not entirely settled.

The opportunity sitting alongside all this is the annual picture. With most of the year now behind you, you have already secured around 94% of the full-year target, leaving a little under ฿900,000 still to sell to hit the year’s number in full. That is a comfortable position this late in the year, and it gives you room to be selective about how the remaining rooms are sold — holding rate rather than discounting to fill them — rather than needing to chase volume.

Looking into the next quarter, the target set is a little higher than this quarter’s, and the softening seen in September’s occupancy and rate is the main signal to watch. If it continues rather than proving a one-month dip, it would be worth understanding whether it reflects the season, the wider market, or something specific to how the hotel is being sold.
